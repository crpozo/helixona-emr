Types and colour codes
The taxonomy behind the calendar — type, subtype, letters and colour, editable in one place
The colour code is data, not styling. A subtype owns its letters and its colour here, and every screen reads them — the block edge on the calendar, the chip in the appointment detail, the legend, and the treatment filter. Change it here and it changes everywhere at once.
Three channels, and they are the clinic’s, not ours. The background is the appointment type, the side bar is which treatment, and a faint watermark over the background is where the patient stands. Every colour on this page comes from the clinic’s own sheet. The status symbols are drawn as inline SVG rather than emoji: an emoji renders in its own colours on top of a palette the clinic chose, and differently on every operating system. Where a colour is used more than once, the other treatments that share it are named — they always sit on a different background, so the pair still reads apart on the board.

Appointment types and subtypes
The taxonomy itself — add one here and it appears in booking, the filters, the legend, the capacity rows and the waitlist at the same moment
A type is a kind of visit; a subtype is the treatment itself. The type owns the background colour on the calendar and, with it, the CAPACITY — an IV chair takes any IV. The subtype owns the side bar and the duration. That is why a subtype colour only has to be unique within its type: the two always sit on different backgrounds. The colours are edited on the codes screen.
